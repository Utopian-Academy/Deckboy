Cues going to air, then effects stacked onto the live picture, then the built-in video synth. Shown with the test patterns Deckboy generates itself.

📽️

Slides, as cues

A PDF, PowerPoint or Keynote deck imports as one cue per slide, speaker notes and all, rendered once on the way in so nothing during the show depends on a document program. Presenter view is an output type in its own right — live slide, the one before, the one after, the notes, the clock — and so is a mirrored prompter that scrolls at a pace you set. Most cue players do not do slides. At all. The usual alternative is somebody’s laptop, a long cable and a prayer.

📡

Five outputs, at once

NDI, SRT, RTMP, Blackmagic SDI and SMPTE ST 2110, several at once, each with its own area of interest, edge feather and warp. Uncompressed IP video is normally the preserve of equipment that arrives with a quotation attached.

🎬

Cues, in order

Drag media in, trim it, fade it, loop it, hold the last frame rather than reveal a desktop. Then a rack of pixel effects on the live cue, every parameter drivable from an oscillator, because sometimes the brief is “can it look a bit broken, but on purpose”.

🏴

Free, and nothing phones home

No account. No dongle. No licence server, no seat count, no trial that expires on the morning of the gig. GPL-3.0, free for good — which is a reliability argument as much as a principle, because a licence check is one more thing that can refuse to start a show at 19:55, in a venue whose wi-fi would first like you to accept its terms and conditions. No telemetry either. Deckboy has never needed the network to play a file, and the only thing it sends anywhere is your video.

🛠️

Audio that can see

Every audio plug-in ever written receives a buffer of samples and nothing else. A compressor in a desk cannot know that the thing it is compressing is a drone shot, that the shot sits a quarter of the way across the screen, or that it has four seconds left. Deckboy keeps the picture and the sound in one object, so five of its fourteen audio effects read the frame they are playing under. Plus EBU R128 normalise, in-app transcoding, and test cards for proving a chain.

Presenter view is an output type of its own, so it takes its own display — live slide, the one before, the one after, the notes and the clock.
