Video, cued

A playlist of clips that go to air when you say so. Drag media in, trim it, fade it, loop it, hold the last frame rather than reveal a desktop. Thirteen ways to get from one cue to the next — cut, crossfade, dip through black or white, pushes, wipes, an iris — set for the deck and overridden on any cue that wants its own. Hardware decoding, frame-accurate transport, and a cue that knows how long it has left. Then a rack of pixel effects on the live picture, every parameter drivable from an oscillator, because sometimes the brief is “can it look a bit broken, but on purpose”.

📡

Out to the whole rig

SRT and RTMP streams, Blackmagic SDI and SMPTE ST 2110, each output with its own area of interest, edge feather and warp. The same programme can go to several outputs while it records, and presenter view takes an output of its own. Uncompressed IP video is normally the preserve of equipment that arrives with a quotation attached.

NDI is in every download, in and out: send any output as an NDI source on the network, or take someone else’s NDI source in as a cue. The NDI runtime comes from your own NDI Tools install, so nothing is bundled that is not ours to give.

A different deck on each output — multi-deck, multi-output playback — is coming in Super Deckboy.

🪟

A window, or a web page, as a cue

Point a cue at any window on the machine — a score bug, a timer, a rehearsal tool, a spreadsheet somebody insists on — and it arrives at the window’s own full resolution whatever your display scaling is set to, scaled to fit the frame. It follows the window as it moves and resizes, and anything in front of it stays out of shot.

Point one at a URL instead and the page renders inside the programme, not in a browser window on your desktop. A dashboard, a lyric page or a live scoreboard is a cue like any other, with the same fades, the same effects and the same take button.

Slides, as cues

A PDF, PowerPoint or Keynote deck imports as one cue per slide, speaker notes and all, rendered once on the way in so nothing during the show depends on a document program. Presenter view is an output type in its own right — live slide, the one before, the one after, the notes, the clock — and so is a mirrored teleprompter that scrolls at a pace you set. Most cue players do not do slides. At all. The usual alternative is somebody’s laptop, a long cable and a prayer.

Free, and nothing phones home

No account. No dongle. No licence server, no seat count, no trial that expires on the morning of the gig. GPL-3.0, free for good — which is a reliability argument as much as a principle, because a licence check is one more thing that can refuse to start a show at 19:55, in a venue whose wi-fi would first like you to accept its terms and conditions. No telemetry either. Deckboy has never needed the network to play a file, and the only thing it sends anywhere is your video.

🎛️

36 video effects, 14 audio, 5 you cannot get anywhere else

A per-cue rack on the live picture — displacement, feedback, reaction–diffusion, pixel sort, datamosh, Chladni plates — with every parameter drivable from an oscillator. Timed one at a time at 1080p on a laptop processor, the heaviest we have measured takes three-quarters of a 60fps frame, and most take under half.

Then the five only Deckboy has, because they need the picture and the sound in one object and a plug-in only ever gets samples: Picture (the video drives the filter), Placement (where the shot sits on the raster is where it sits in the room), Seam (the end of the cue resolves the tail), Frame lock (stutter quantised to the frame period) and Suspend (a held cue keeps its room tone). How it holds the frame budget.

Databend reads the frame out as a signal and plays it through an audio chain — a delay with feedback, a filter, a wavefolder — then paints what comes back. The look people chase by opening a picture in an audio editor, on a live cue and on a knob. Its opposite number, audioprint, draws the sound the deck has just played through the picture; put one of each on a cue and they bend each other.

Presenter view is an output type of its own, so it takes its own display. Here it is part-way through a build: the live slide holds still while the notes advance a step at a time, the part already spoken greys out, and the footer counts what is left before the next cue.
