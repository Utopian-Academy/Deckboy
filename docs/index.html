Cues, in order

Drag media in, trim it, fade it, loop it, hold the last frame. Then a rack of pixel effects on the live cue, each parameter drivable from an oscillator, because sometimes the brief is “can it look a bit broken, but on purpose”.

📡

Five outputs, at once

NDI, SRT, RTMP, Blackmagic SDI and SMPTE ST 2110, several at once, plus warp and edge blend per output. An unreasonable quantity of protocol for what is, at heart, a box that plays telly.

🎛️

Slides, with the notes

A PDF, PowerPoint or Keynote deck imports as one cue per slide, speaker notes and all. Presenter view is an output type in its own right — live slide, previous, next, notes, clock — and so is a mirrored prompter that scrolls at a pace you set. Most cue players do not do slides. At all.

🛠️

Audio that can see

Every audio plug-in ever written receives a buffer of samples and nothing else. A compressor in a desk cannot know that the thing it is compressing is a drone shot, that the shot sits a quarter of the way across the screen, or that it has four seconds left. Deckboy keeps the picture and the sound in one object, so five of its fourteen audio effects read the frame they are playing under. Plus EBU R128 normalise, in-app transcoding, and test cards for proving a chain.

Presenter view is an output type of its own, so it takes its own display — live slide, the one before, the one after, the notes and the clock.
