How Deckboy compares

The free one, against nine others — price, platform and what you get.

Deckboy is a cue-based video playback deck: a playlist of clips, slides, pages, cameras and feeds that go to air when the operator says so, out to screens, NDI, SDI and streams, while it records. These are the nine tools it sits beside on a show, asked the same five questions.

What the free download has in it

  • One cue list for every source: clips, stills, PDF, PowerPoint and Keynote slides, web pages, any window, cameras, NDI, SRT, RTMP and SDI input
  • Every output at once, while it records: screens, NDI, Blackmagic SDI with key and fill, SMPTE ST 2110 with NMOS and PTP, SRT and RTMP
  • A real running order: standby and GO, pre-waits, continues, arm and disarm, audition, preload and a pre-show CHECK
  • 42 video effects and 23 audio effects on any cue, every dial on an oscillator, plus your own VST3 plugins
  • Projection mapping per layer, in perspective, dragged on the programme monitor
  • Lower thirds, stage timers, presenter and prompter views, each an output of its own
  • Driven from anything: Stream Deck, Companion, MIDI Show Control, OSC, Art-Net, LTC, HyperDeck, the vMix API
  • Windows, macOS and Linux, from one show file

The thing the table is really saying

Deckboy is the only one that runs on all three systems, and the only one that costs nothing. Six of the others are macOS only and two are Windows only; Resolume runs on both, and none of them runs on Linux. If the machine going to the venue is not the machine the software wants, that is the end of the conversation before features are even discussed.

Then there is the second machine. Most of the others are licensed by the computer, so a bigger rig means more licences, and several are rented by the week or the month, or buy a year of updates and then ask again. Deckboy goes on the main machine, the backup and the laptop in the flight case for the same price: nothing. QLab has a free tier, but it only runs on a Mac, and the features a real show reaches for are licensed or rented by the day on top.

Deckboy is free. Not a trial, not a watermark, not a feature-limited tier with the useful half behind a licence server: the whole application, on all three operating systems, under the GPL. The spare machine in the flight case can run it too, and so can the one you are reading this on.

What most of them do not give you

Not "does it better" — mostly does not have. Every item here is in the free download, on all three platforms:

  • Your own VST3 effects and instruments, inside a cue. The reverb you already own or the channel strip your mix is built around sits in that cue's chain beside Deckboy's own effects, with the plugin's controls on the rows beneath it and its settings saved in the show. A plugin that cannot keep up with its block is taken out and reported, rather than clicking through the PA.
  • Any window on the machine, as a cue. A score bug, a timer, a rehearsal tool, the spreadsheet somebody insists on — at the window's own resolution whatever the display scaling is, following it as it moves, with anything in front of it kept out of shot, so the operator carries on working on the same box. How that works.
  • A web page as a cue, rendered inside the programme rather than in a browser window you have to keep on top.
  • Effects that are instruments rather than filters: schlieren imaging, Chladni figures, a real wave equation, crystal grain growth, retinal persistence, structure-tensor grain flow — and two that cross between picture and sound in both directions.
  • A test-pattern generator and a screen-check tool in the same application that plays the show, so the wall can be proved before anyone arrives.
  • It runs with no sound card at all — a rack PC feeding a video wall opens, plays and puts a picture out.
  • One show file on Windows, macOS and Linux. None of the others runs on Linux, and most run on one operating system only.

Where Deckboy fits

  • The job is playing video cues, reliably, on the night. A playlist of clips with trim, fades, loops and hold-last-frame, hardware-accelerated decode, and a cue that knows how long it has left, and thirteen cue-to-cue transition styles — cut, crossfade, dips, four pushes, four wipes and an iris. If a client sends a file that will not play, it converts in place without leaving the application; if they send one mastered too quietly, it normalises to EBU R128. That is the day job, and everything below is what happens after it is handled.
  • The show machine is a PC, or a mixture. "QLab for Windows" and "Mitti for Windows" are common searches for a reason. Deckboy runs natively on Windows, macOS and Linux from the same show file.
  • The signal has to leave in more than one shape. NDI, SRT, RTMP, Blackmagic SDI and SMPTE ST 2110 can run at once, so the playback machine often solves the conversion problem on the day as well.
  • It has to be driven from a surface, or from the lighting desk. A Stream Deck plugin and a Bitfocus Companion module, plus OSC, MIDI, Art-Net and LTC timecode. MIDI Show Control lets an Eos or a grandMA fire cues by number, which is how a theatre keeps video on the same GO as the lights. MIDI is in every download.
  • Slides are part of the show. PDF, PowerPoint and Keynote import as one cue per slide with speaker notes, and presenter and prompter views are output types of their own. Most cue players do not handle slides at all.
  • Something on the machine has to go to air. A window on the show PC can be a cue — a score bug, a timer, a rehearsal tool, the spreadsheet somebody insists on — at the window’s own resolution whatever the display scaling is, following it as it moves, with anything in front of it kept out of shot, so the operator can carry on working on the same box. A web page can be a cue too, rendered inside the programme rather than in a browser window you have to keep on top. Most cue players send you to a capture card or a second machine for either.
  • The sound needs to follow the picture. Five of the twenty-three per-cue audio effects read the frame they are playing under — its brightness, where it sits in the raster, how long it has left. A plug-in in a desk receives samples and nothing else, so it cannot. Two effects cross the other way as well: databend plays the picture through an audio chain and paints what comes back, and audioprint draws the sound the deck has just played through the picture.
  • Budget is zero. GPL-3.0, and nothing phones home.
