Quick answers

Interlaced or progressive?

Progressive draws every line of every frame. Interlaced draws the odd lines, then the even lines, as two fields. 1080i is interlaced, 1080p progressive. Shown on a screen that expects progressive without deinterlacing, interlaced video shows comb-like teeth on anything that moves.

What is EDID?

A small block of data a screen sends back up the cable saying which resolutions, rates and audio it accepts. The source picks a format from that list. Splitters, converters and extenders can pass on the wrong list, which is why a picture vanishes when one is put in the chain. An EDID emulator holds one fixed list.

Dr. Pixel · VID 230

What is HDCP?

Copy protection on HDMI and DisplayPort. Protected content (streaming apps, Blu-ray, some laptops' whole screen) only passes to devices that pass the HDCP handshake. SDI has no HDCP, so a protected picture will not go out over SDI. It is a separate thing from EDID.

Fibre, SDI or HDMI?

HDMI is made for short runs from a source to a screen, and plugs that lock are rare. SDI uses locking BNC connectors on 75-ohm coax and runs much further; each rate (3G, 12G) has its own distance limit in the cable maker's table. Fibre runs furthest and carries no electrical noise between buildings, but needs the right transceivers and clean connectors.

Why 59.94 and not 60?

When colour was added to American television in 1953, the rate was lowered by 1000/1001 to keep the colour signal from interfering with the sound. Equipment still offers both, and a mismatch between them can drop or repeat a frame every so often.

Full range or legal range?

Video usually keeps black at 16 and white at 235 (in 8-bit); computer screens use 0 to 255. Mix them up and the picture looks washed out (grey blacks) or crushed (lost shadows and highlights). Set both ends to agree.
