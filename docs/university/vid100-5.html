How much data is that?

PROF. VIDEO

Now the arithmetic. It is easy, and it explains almost everything in this university.

Each pixel has three numbers. In 8-bit video, each number takes one byte. So one pixel is 3 bytes. Multiply by the pixels. Multiply by the frames. That is how much data video is, before anybody makes it smaller.

PROF. VIDEO

Look at one hour of 1080p at 30 fps. That is about 670 GB, and a show has more than one hour of video. At 4K, one hour is more than 2.5 TB. A good network cable carries 1 gigabit each second, and this needs about 1.5 gigabits for 1080p at 30 fps.

So we throw things away. Carefully. A codec is the tool that does this. It stores less data and rebuilds a picture that looks nearly the same. H.264 is a codec. HEVC and AV1 are codecs. That is a whole course, too.

DR. PIXEL

Watch two things, skapi. A byte is 8 bits. Disks use bytes: MB and GB. Networks use bits: Mb and Gb, or Mbps and Gbps. A small b or a big B changes the answer by eight times.

And a file is not a codec. An .mp4 or a .mov is a container: a box that holds the video and the sound. Two .mp4 files can have different codecs inside. That is why one plays and the other does not.
