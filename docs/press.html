Copy, ready to paste

One line

Deckboy is a free, open-source video cue deck for Windows, macOS and Linux: clips, slides and live sources in one running order.

One paragraph

Deckboy puts video clips, stills, slide decks and live sources in one cue list. An operator selects the next cue and takes it to a separate programme output, with fades, loops and hold-last-frame. Send the programme to a screen, over NDI, or to an SRT or RTMP stream, and record it while it runs. A Stream Deck, Bitfocus Companion, OSC, MIDI or timecode can drive the show. Deckboy runs on Windows, macOS and Linux and is free under GPL-3.0-or-later, with no account, subscription or telemetry.

Choose the workflow

  • For event and theatre operators: select the next clip or slide while the audience sees the programme output. Start with live events or theatre.
  • For video engineers: send a cue list to an NDI receiver, or bring an NDI source into the list. Start with NDI input and output.
  • For streaming crews: take cues to an SRT or RTMP output and keep a local programme recording. See outputs and recording.

NDI needs the NDI runtime supplied with NDI Tools. SDI needs compatible Blackmagic hardware and its driver. The manual describes source, output and platform requirements.

More to explore

Deckboy hosts your own VST3 effects and instruments inside a cue's audio chain, so the reverb or channel strip an engineer already owns works on a cue beside Deckboy's own effects, and an instrument plays from a MIDI keyboard or the computer's. A window cue takes any application on the show machine to air at its own full resolution, following it as it moves, with anything in front of it kept out of shot — so the operator carries on working on the same computer. A rack of effects built from physics rather than filters: schlieren imaging, Chladni figures, a wave equation, crystal grain growth, retinal persistence. And a pair that crosses between picture and sound — one plays the picture through an audio chain, the other draws the played audio through the picture.

Things people get wrong

  • It is not a switcher. It is a source and a player; it pairs with a switcher rather than replacing one.
  • It is not a projection-mapping suite, though it has warp, keystone and edge blend per output.
  • There is no paid version, no "pro" tier and no upgrade path. This is the whole application.
  • The builds are not code-signed. Windows and macOS may show a notice on first run; follow the platform-specific instructions on the release page.
