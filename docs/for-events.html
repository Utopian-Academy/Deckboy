One screen,
everything on it

The keynote, the walk-in loop, the sponsor video, the live poll and the speaker's countdown, from one list and one spacebar, instead of five programs and a lot of alt-tab.

A PDF slide with the stage timer layered small in the corner, then a live web page taken like a clip while the clock keeps running. Recorded from Deckboy 0.99.397. Music: YMCK, used with permission · vertical version

The things an event actually plays

  • Slides. A PDF, PowerPoint or Keynote deck becomes one cue per slide, with its notes. A presenter view takes its own screen.
  • Web pages. A live poll, a scoreboard or a social wall is rendered into the programme, not shown as a browser window on your desktop.
  • Video and stills, hardware-decoded, trimmed and faded.
  • A stage timer, full screen or small in a corner over whatever is up.
  • Lower thirds for speakers, in thirteen looks.

Recorded while it plays

The programme is recorded as it goes out, to H.264, HEVC, ProRes or DNxHR, while it also goes to the screens and, if you need it, to a stream.

On the machine you already have

Windows, macOS or Linux, with no account and no licence server, so the spare laptop in the flight case can be a second show machine without a second licence.
